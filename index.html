bio

Sasha Ricci Rovatti works in moving image, graphic design and text. Her practice often takes as a focus the ways histories are narrativised and shaped by visual politics.

Graduated with a bachelor's degree in Graphic Design and Visual Communication from ISIA Urbino in 2023, she worked in Bologna and Foligno in design for publishing. She then pursued a master's degree in the Critical Inquiry Lab department of the Design Academy Eindhoven, where she graduated with an award nominee project on past militant Italian cinema.

Synopsis


“The Wretched of the Screen” revisits the legacy and contradictions of 20th-century Italian communist filmmakers to question how political images might still generate solidarity today. Inspired by the recent strikes for Palestine in Italy, and in dialogue with Valentino Orsini’s “I dannati della terra” (“The Wretched of the Earth”), it explores reenactment as a cinematic and political practice, examining the responsibilities and contradictions of engaging with anti-colonial struggles from historical and contemporary Western perspectives. Developed through archival research, fieldwork and in collaboration with the Audiovisual Archive of the Labour and Democratic Movement (IT), it combines historical footage, newly filmed material and text through editing strategies that treat the archive as an unfinished conversation rather than a finalised record. By revisiting the visual legacy of anti-colonial cinema, the piece asks who has the authority to represent liberation struggles while recovering the internationalist hope embedded within these histories.

Synopsis


“To hold the world under our tongue” brings together critical texts that examine infrastructures in an expansive way. The publication features contributions by Justin Ackerschott, Adam Białek, Jason-Albert Degleri- s,Emilia Doohan, Gary Duvernay, Elena Grippo, Emma Itria, Elizabeth Kruse, Léontine Maniak, Bianca Maria Negrini, Juliët Nijland, Gittit Shwartz and myself. I was in charge of the conceptual and graphic design of the publication as well as the graphics for social communication of the launch event.

My contribution, “All the Images Will Disappear Until I Look at Them,” draws on critical literature on family history to engage with colonial history and reflect on how the colonial past is remembered, forgotten, and imaginatively reconstructed.


Excerpt


[…] On the side, between the sun-faded wooden planks that cover the truck, in capital letters I read: AUTOCOLONNA ROVATTI. Autocolonna means convoy, caravan. Rovatti is the surname of my grandfather, my mother and myself. Each time I look at the image, I’m startled by this connection. I freeze in the moment of recognition, there is a line that joined him to me but our eyes will never meet. Although the image of this man is disembodied for me, without any voice, smell or gesture, after all, I never knew him and was not given any family anecdotes or mythologies about him, it seems to me that the image is filled with a dizzying proximity which makes the very process of remembering an essentially political act.

Synopsis


“The Place of Dum Palm” interrogates the symbolic use of landscape within Italian colonial discourse: what words were used to evoke the light, the weight of air, nature framing colonization as a natural, even inevitable, extension of the landscape itself? In the late nineteenth century, during the early phase of Italy’s occupation of Eritrea, the Dum palm, regarded as the most important plant of the local flora by Italian enterprises, was subject to extraction for local exploitation and export. In 2025, in a greenhouse in Amsterdam, I confronted a specimen of Doum palm. “What do your fibers, your roots, your trunk remember?”

From this encounter, a series of discards, micro-fractures, and glitches emerge in the frame to haunt the present, bringing to the surface fragments of that rhetorical discourse.


Screened in the collective exhibition “Some Matters Do Not Sattle” hosted in Saloon Veneman gallery, Eindhoven. I was in charge of the graphics for social communication of the opening event and all the print matter.
